Plan · The index cards

Outline and Generate scenes

Plan a chapter as scenes before you write it.

How it works

  1. Open the Outline beside any page.
  2. Say what happens next, choose how many scenes you want, and press Generate scenes.
  3. Edit, reorder, add or delete scenes until the plan is right.

Good for knowing where a chapter is going before you start it.

Plan · The stack of paper

Draft Page

A first pass at the chapter, written from your scenes.

How it works

  1. With scenes in the Outline, press Draft Page.
  2. In Generation settings, choose Pages to generate and Words per page.
  3. Read the draft and rework it like any page. Or write the chapter yourself, scenes beside you.

Good for getting past the blank page on a chapter you have already planned.

Plan · The corkboard

The Canvas

Your story world as linked cards, beside the manuscript.

How it works

  1. Add cards from the tray: Character, Location, Plot, Item, Faction or Note.
  2. Draw a line between two cards and say how they relate.
  3. Select a card and Branch to grow a related one. Feldar reads the board before it writes.

Good for keeping names, places and who knows what straight across a long book.

How the Canvas works

Write · The typewriter

Continue from here

The next lines, when they will not come.

How it works

  1. Hover over the caret at the end of a paragraph.
  2. Choose Continue from here, or Extend paragraph, Add transition or Start dialogue.
  3. The lines arrive in blue. Accept them, Revise them, or Dismiss them.

Good for getting unstuck mid scene without handing over the scene.

The first commercially successful typewriter gave writers the QWERTY keys. The one on this desk types in a second color, and it is blue.

How Feldar's AI behaves

Write · The quill and ink

Polish, Deepen, Enhance

Rewrite a passage and see every change.

How it works

  1. Select a passage.
  2. Choose Polish, Deepen or Enhance, or type what you want with Instruct.
  3. The card shows the rewrite with the added words marked. Apply keeps it, and Undo takes it back.

Good for line editing in your own voice.

Rewriting in your voice

Write · The handwritten page

Voice & Style

Help that sounds like you.

How it works

  1. Open Settings, then Voice & Style.
  2. Paste a sample of your own prose, at least 600 words.
  3. Feldar writes toward it, and follows the book's own prose as the project grows.

Good for suggestions that sound like the rest of your book.

AI for novelists

Revise · The red pencil

Checks

Five editing passes, with a fix for every mark.

How it works

  1. Open Checks beside the page. Opening it changes nothing.
  2. Run the passes: Fix critical mistakes, Improve accuracy and clarity, Tighten word choice, Smooth dialogue transitions, Clarify character perspectives.
  3. Each issue is marked in the text. Accept the fix, ask Rewrite for me, or Discard it.

Good for a clean-up pass before anyone else reads the chapter.

All features

Revise · The six portraits

Lens

Six readers beside your page.

How it works

  1. Open Chat and switch to Lens.
  2. Ask the Critic, Cheerleader, Poet, Editor, Plotter or Reader, or @mention one.
  3. Let them answer one by one, or set them to Debate and Synthesize.

Good for hearing what works, and what does not, before the draft goes out.

Readers once wrote their comments in the margins of handwritten books. The notes are called glosses. Lens puts the readers beside your page instead.

All features

Revise · The reading lamp

Reader reactions

How an audience might take the chapter.

How it works

  1. In Lens, choose Reader reactions.
  2. Pick an audience: General readers, Fantasy readers, Book club readers, Creative writing students or Literary magazine editors.
  3. Read the report, with markers in the margin of your page.

Good for seeing a chapter the way a particular audience might.

Keep · The clay tablet

Writing record

A private record of how the book was written.

How it works

  1. Write as you normally do.
  2. Open Writing record from the Options menu.
  3. See the words you typed by hand and the words that came through Feldar, with active days, active time and models used.

Good for showing a publisher, a prize or a workshop how the book was made.

The first writing we know of, from about 3300 BCE, was a record too: accounts of grain, beer and livestock, pressed into wet clay with a reed.

Keep · The bookshelf

Library, import and export

Every book on one shelf, and free to leave.

How it works

  1. Each project gets a cover in your Library.
  2. Import a manuscript as Word, PDF, Markdown or plain text, up to 20 MB a file.
  3. Export the whole book or chosen pages in the same four formats.

Good for bringing a draft in, and taking it out whenever you like.

Keep · The radio

Choose the model

Six models, chosen per feature.

How it works

  1. In Settings, pick a model for each feature, or switch models in Chat.
  2. Choose from GPT-6 Astra, Sol and Luna, and Claude Opus 5.5, Sonnet 5.5 and Haiku 5.5.

Models by plan

The tools keep changing. The words stay yours.

Clay, papyrus, parchment, type, keys and screens: each made writing a little easier, and none of them wrote the book. Feldar is the newest tool on the desk. Its help arrives in blue, and stays only if you accept it.

House rules

  • Your page changes when you say so.

    Suggestions wait in blue or in a card. Dismiss one and the page is as you left it.

  • Your writing trains nothing.

    Feldar does not train AI models on your writing.

  • Your book is yours.

    You own what you write. Export the whole book whenever you like.

  • You can see what came from where.

    Blue lines and the Writing record show which words came through Feldar.
